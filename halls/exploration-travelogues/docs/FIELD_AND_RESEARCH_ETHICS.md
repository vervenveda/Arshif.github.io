# Field & Research Ethics

## Core Principle
Travel and fieldwork do not create entitlement to another person's privacy, grief, culture, sacred place, labor, story, image or knowledge.

## Field Observation
Record:
- date/time;
- location/route;
- weather/conditions;
- direct observations;
- measurements where appropriate;
- uncertainty;
- source follow-up;
- privacy/consent restrictions.

## Living Cultural Heritage
Community access rules and customary restrictions should be respected.

UNESCO's ethical principles for intangible cultural heritage emphasize community primacy, mutual respect, transparent collaboration, informed consent, customary access rules and community benefit.

Official reference:
https://ich.unesco.org/en/ethics-and-ich-00866

## Tourism
Travel analysis should consider:
- environmental impact;
- local economies;
- labor;
- accessibility;
- cultural heritage;
- privacy;
- vulnerable communities;
- visitor behavior;
- who receives benefits and who bears costs.

UN Tourism ethics reference:
https://www.unwto.org/global-code-of-ethics-for-tourism

## Forced Movement
Do not use the language of adventure or exploration for slavery, captivity, deportation, exile, forced migration or displacement unless critically analyzing that framing.

Prioritize testimony, historical context, coercive power, survival, resistance and descendant memory.

## Maps
Maps are evidence and arguments, not neutral decoration.
Document creator, date, audience, scale, projection, labels, data/source, uncertainty and omissions.
