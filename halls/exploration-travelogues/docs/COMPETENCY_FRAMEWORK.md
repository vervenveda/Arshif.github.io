# Spatial-Humanities Competency Framework

## 1. ⌖ Place, Route & Spatial Thinking

Locate, describe and analyze routes, nodes, regions, landscapes, spatial relationships and geographic scale.

## 2. ▧ Maps, Cartography & Geospatial Representation

Read maps as designed representations; analyze projection, scale, labels, selection, blank space, data and purpose.

## 3. § Primary Sources & Source Criticism

Distinguish observation, testimony, report, memory, compilation, translation, edition, propaganda, archive and later scholarship.

## 4. ✍ Travel Writing, Narrative & Rhetoric

Analyze narrator, audience, genre, sensory detail, persona, pacing, description, evidence and revision.

## 5. ⚑ Empire, Power & Counter-Archive

Analyze conquest, coloniality, sponsorship, guides, labor, missing voices, resistance and the politics of representation.

## 6. ⇄ Culture, Translation & Ethical Representation

Use specific cultural context; track mediation and translation; respect privacy, sacred boundaries, consent and living communities.

## 7. ↝ Mobility, Migration & Forced Movement

Distinguish pilgrimage, trade, diaspora, tourism, migration, expedition, exile, captivity, slavery and displacement.

## 8. 🌿 Environment, Field Observation & Change

Document place, weather, water, species, materials, uncertainty and environmental history through repeatable observation.

## 9. ◎ Networks, Scale & Comparative Geography

Compare routes and places across local, regional, national and global scales without flattening difference.

## 10. ◫ Research, Data, Citation & GIS Methods

Develop research questions, evaluate sources/data, document provenance, cite, map and build transparent spatial evidence.

## 11. ✦ Synthesis, Public Atlas & Capstone

Integrate maps, writing, sources, ethics, fieldwork and research into a substantial public-facing or scholarly project.

The Hall intentionally does not calculate one composite 'exploration score.'
