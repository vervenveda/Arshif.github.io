# Travelogues & Exploration 101–603 Course Catalog

## Travelogues 101 · Place, Route, Map & Travelogue Foundations

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 6

**Assigned resource modules:** What Is a Travelogue?; Maps Are Arguments; Ancient Inquiry: Herodotus and the World as Story; Walking, Slowness, and Local Travel

### Outcomes
- Define travelogue, route, itinerary, map, observation, evidence, audience, and point of view.
- Read maps as selected representations rather than neutral mirrors of reality.
- Distinguish direct observation, reported information, memory, inference, and imagination.
- Create a local route journal using sensory evidence and sourced historical context.

**Evidence of learning:** Route sketch · map analysis · source/evidence card · local field journal

**Alignment position:** Grade 6 history/social-studies literacy plus National Geography map/spatial-thinking foundations.

## Travelogues 102 · Pilgrimage, Diaspora & Living Route Memory

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 7

**Assigned resource modules:** Sacred Geography: Pilgrims and Holy Places; Buddhist Routes Across Asia; Jewish Diaspora Itineraries; Women Pilgrims and Women Travelers; Oral Routes: Griots, Wayfinders, and Living Memory

### Outcomes
- Compare pilgrimage, diaspora, oral route memory, and gendered mobility.
- Explain how sacred geography, hospitality, memory, language, and access shape journeys.
- Use specific cultural and community contexts instead of generic travel stereotypes.
- Compare written, oral, mapped, and performed route knowledge.

**Evidence of learning:** Pilgrimage/route comparison · oral-route analysis · map annotation · seminar

**Alignment position:** Grade 7 comparison, point of view, source integration, cultural context, and geographic representations.

## Travelogues 103 · Networks, Oceans & Connected Worlds

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 8

**Assigned resource modules:** The Silk Road as Network, Not Road; Indian Ocean Worlds; Islamic Golden Age Travel Knowledge; Marvels, Monsters, and Imaginary Travel

### Outcomes
- Analyze the Silk Roads and Indian Ocean as networks rather than single paths.
- Connect ports, monsoons, trade, religion, language, technology, and political power.
- Distinguish observed travel from marvel literature, compilation, rumor, and imagined geography.
- Use maps, routes, and textual evidence together.

**Evidence of learning:** Network map · maritime route worksheet · source-reliability analysis · comparative response

**Alignment position:** Grade 8 strongest-evidence, source comparison, spatial organization, research, and map interpretation.

## Travelogues 201 · Empire, Encounter, Conquest & Counter-Archive

**Level:** High School · Grades 9–10

**Assigned resource modules:** Printing Voyages: Hakluyt and the Promotion of Empire; Atlantic Encounter and Catastrophe; Shipwreck and Dependency: Cabeza de Vaca; Conquest Chronicles and Counter-Archives; Africa in Travel Writing: Agency, Guides, and Routes

### Outcomes
- Analyze how travel writing can support empire, conquest, commerce, advocacy, resistance, and memory.
- Compare colonial travel accounts with counter-sources and missing/local voices.
- Evaluate date, origin, purpose, audience, mediation, and power in historical sources.
- Write evidence-based analysis without romanticizing colonial violence.

**Evidence of learning:** Colonial-source audit · counter-archive · comparative essay · map/source seminar

**Alignment position:** Grades 9–10 history/social-studies source analysis, point-of-view comparison, evidence, and formal writing.

## Travelogues 202 · Scientific Travel, Rivers & Environmental Observation

**Level:** High School · Grades 9–10

**Assigned resource modules:** Scientific Travel: Humboldt, Darwin, Wallace; Botany, Art, and the Traveling Eye; River Travel: Mississippi, Nile, Niger, Amazon; Travel Writing and Environmental History

### Outcomes
- Analyze scientific travel as observation, measurement, illustration, collection, classification, and environmental history.
- Compare river routes and ecological systems across regions.
- Separate reproducible observation from aesthetic impression and inherited assumption.
- Create a field notebook with methods, uncertainty, source notes, and environmental context.

**Evidence of learning:** Field notebook · river-system comparison · natural-history source critique · environmental route study

**Alignment position:** Grades 9–10 research/source integration with geographic and scientific observation.

## Travelogues 203 · Tourism, Journalism, Polar Myth & Ethics of Movement

**Level:** High School · Grades 11–12

**Assigned resource modules:** Tourism, Guidebooks, and the Standardized Gaze; Journalism and Speed: Nellie Bly; Polar Exploration and Heroic Myth; Forced Journeys: Slavery, Exile, Displacement; Sacred Boundaries and Travel Ethics; Translation, Mediation, and Who Edits the Journey

### Outcomes
- Analyze tourism, journalism, expedition narrative, forced movement, translation, editing, and sacred boundaries.
- Evaluate whose mobility is voluntary, restricted, privileged, coerced, or erased.
- Assess claims, reasoning, evidence, translation, editorial mediation, and archival gaps.
- Produce a sustained ethical analysis of a route, journey, destination, or travel text.

**Evidence of learning:** Tourism-impact audit · forced-journey source study · mediation analysis · 6–8 page research paper

**Alignment position:** Grades 11–12 source synthesis, competing explanations, evidence, uncertainty, research, and formal argument.

## Travelogues 301 · Advanced Cartography, Scale & Spatial Source Analysis

**Level:** Advanced High School · AP / IB Geography Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Maps Are Arguments; The Silk Road as Network, Not Road; Indian Ocean Worlds; River Travel: Mississippi, Nile, Niger, Amazon; Translation, Mediation, and Who Edits the Journey; Local Archive Expedition

**Alignment position:** Supports AP Human Geography skill categories in spatial relationships, data/source analysis and scale; also supports IB Geography methods.

## Travelogues 302 · Travel Writing, Testimony & Ethical Counter-Narrative

**Level:** Advanced High School · Literature / History Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Women Pilgrims and Women Travelers; Atlantic Encounter and Catastrophe; Shipwreck and Dependency: Cabeza de Vaca; Conquest Chronicles and Counter-Archives; Africa in Travel Writing: Agency, Guides, and Routes; Tourism, Guidebooks, and the Standardized Gaze; Forced Journeys: Slavery, Exile, Displacement; Sacred Boundaries and Travel Ethics; Comparative Route Study; Build an Ethical Dossier

### Outcomes
- Read travel writing simultaneously as literature, testimony, historical source, geographic representation, and power relation.
- Compare traveler, guide, host, translator, editor, colonized, displaced, and descendant perspectives.
- Build an ethical counter-archive without inventing missing testimony.
- Develop a comparative route study and documented dossier.

**Evidence of learning:** Close reading · counter-archive · comparative route study · ethical dossier · oral defense

**Alignment position:** Advanced humanities source criticism, argument, comparison, research, and ethical representation.

## Travelogues 303 · Field Methods, Local Geography & Travelogue Writing

**Assigned resource modules:** Scientific Travel: Humboldt, Darwin, Wallace; Botany, Art, and the Traveling Eye; Walking, Slowness, and Local Travel; Travel Writing and Environmental History; Local Archive Expedition; Travelogue Writing Workshop; Capstone: The World as Archive

**Level:** Graduate-Style · Thesis / Capstone

**Assigned resource modules:** Comparative Route Study; Build an Ethical Dossier; Travelogue Writing Workshop; Capstone: The World as Archive

### Outcomes
- Define an original, bounded question about route, mobility, travel writing, field observation, mapping, environment, or archive.

- Defend a critical, digital, literary, geographic, or hybrid capstone and document its public-use boundaries.

**Alignment position:** Highest Khaemenes Travelogues Hall pathway; graduate-style independent work, not an accredited thesis.
