# Philosophy 101–603 Course Catalog

## Philosophy 101 · Questions, Reasons & the Examined Life

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 6

**Assigned resource modules:** What Is Philosophy?; Tools of Reasoning: Logic, Dialogue, and Argument; Socrates and the Examined Life; Hellenistic Schools: Stoics, Epicureans, Skeptics, Cynics

### Outcomes
- Distinguish philosophical questions from simple factual questions.
- Identify claims, premises, conclusions, reasons, and respectful objections.
- Use Socratic questioning to test assumptions without turning disagreement into attack.
- Connect philosophical ideas to habits, choices, friendship, courage, happiness, and civic life.

**Evidence of learning:** Question journal · argument map · Socratic dialogue · short reflection · oral discussion

**Alignment position:** Grade 6 argument writing, evidence, source reading, discussion, and introductory philosophical reasoning.

## Philosophy 102 · Virtue, Society & Ancient World Wisdom

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 7

**Assigned resource modules:** Ancient African & Near Eastern Wisdom; The Pre-Socratics: Nature, Change, and the First Principles; Plato: Forms, Justice, Soul, and the Cave; Aristotle: Nature, Virtue, Logic, and the Good Life; Roman Moral Philosophy: Duty, Law, Empire, and Inner Rule; Chinese Foundations: Confucian, Daoist, Mohist, and Legalist Worlds; Confucius, Mencius, and Xunzi: Humaneness and Human Nature; African Philosophies: Ma'at, Ubuntu, Akan, Yoruba, Sage Wisdom

### Outcomes
- Compare ideas of justice, virtue, order, flourishing, duty, and personhood.
- Use specific texts/traditions rather than treating world philosophy as one universal doctrine.
- Explain how philosophical ideas arise within historical and cultural contexts.
- Write a comparison supported by accurate textual or source evidence.

**Evidence of learning:** Virtue comparison · source cards · seminar · comparative essay · philosophical proverb analysis

**Alignment position:** Grade 7 evidence, comparison, point of view, explanatory writing, and source-based reasoning.

## Philosophy 103 · Self, Reality, Knowledge & Liberation

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 8

**Assigned resource modules:** Indian Foundations: Veda, Upanishads, Dharma, Karma, Moksha; Buddhist Philosophy: Suffering, Impermanence, No-Self, Compassion; Jain, Nyaya, and Indian Logic: Nonviolence and Many-Sided Truth; Vedanta, Yoga, and Paths of Liberation; Daoism: Dao, Wu Wei, Ziran, and the Limits of Naming; Indigenous Philosophies of the Americas: Land, Relation, Memory, and Responsibility

### Outcomes
- Compare accounts of self, knowledge, reality, suffering, liberation, relation, and responsibility.
- Recognize translation and cultural-category limits in comparative philosophy.
- Develop claims from the strongest relevant public evidence.
- Distinguish disagreement, contradiction, complementarity, and genuinely different conceptual frameworks.

**Evidence of learning:** Concept map · comparative inquiry · source evaluation · seminar presentation · revised argument

**Alignment position:** Grade 8 strongest-evidence, source comparison, argument/research, and increasingly independent inquiry.

## Philosophy 201 · Faith, Reason & Medieval Crossroads

**Level:** High School · Grades 9–10

**Assigned resource modules:** Islamic Philosophy: Falsafa, Kalam, Illumination, and Mystical Reason; Jewish Philosophy: Covenant, Law, Exile, Reason, and Revelation; Christian Patristic and Scholastic Philosophy; Medieval Crossroads: Translation, Commentary, and Global Transmission

### Outcomes
- Analyze relationships among reason, revelation, law, metaphysics, commentary, translation, and institutions.
- Trace philosophical ideas across Arabic, Hebrew, Latin, Greek, Persian, and other intellectual networks.
- Evaluate arguments fairly before criticizing them.
- Write claims and counterclaims using relevant evidence.

**Evidence of learning:** Translation/commentary map · argument reconstruction · comparative seminar · analytical essay

**Alignment position:** Grades 9–10 argument, counterclaim, source integration, research, and complex conceptual reading.

## Philosophy 202 · Early Modern Knowledge, Skepticism & Humanism

**Level:** High School · Grades 9–10

**Assigned resource modules:** Renaissance Humanism, Skepticism, and New Worlds; Early Modern Rationalism: Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz; Early Modern Empiricism: Locke, Berkeley, Hume

### Outcomes
- Compare rationalist, empiricist, skeptical, and humanist approaches to knowledge.
- Analyze assumptions about mind, body, experience, identity, and certainty.
- Reconstruct a philosopher's argument before evaluating it.
- Revise philosophical writing for clarity, sequence, evidence, and precision.

**Evidence of learning:** Doubt journal · rationalism/empiricism comparison · argument essay · PROSE revision pass

**Alignment position:** Grades 9–10 substantive argument, evidence, explanatory writing, revision, and disciplined research.

## Philosophy 203 · Rights, Liberty, Justice & Moral Theory

**Level:** High School · Grades 11–12

**Assigned resource modules:** Enlightenment and Political Philosophy: Rights, Contract, Revolution, Critique; Utilitarianism, Liberalism, and Reform; Feminist Philosophy, Gender, Care, and Embodied Knowledge; Environmental and Animal Philosophy: Earth, Kinship, and Moral Community; Aesthetics: Art, Beauty, Taste, Symbol, and the Sacred

### Outcomes
- Compare rights, utility, care, virtue, liberty, environmental responsibility, and aesthetic value.
- Develop precise claims while treating counterarguments fairly and thoroughly.
- Evaluate exclusions and historical limits inside philosophical universal claims.
- Synthesize primary and secondary sources into sustained philosophical writing.

**Evidence of learning:** Ethical case analysis · debate · source synthesis · 6–8 page philosophy paper

**Alignment position:** Grades 11–12 argument, counterargument, source synthesis, sustained research, formal style, and complex interpretation.

## Philosophy 301 · Kant, Idealism, Existentialism & Interpretation

**Level:** Advanced High School · IB / College Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Kant: Critique, Duty, Freedom, and the Conditions of Experience; German Idealism and Romanticism: Spirit, History, Freedom, and Art; Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, and Existentialism; Phenomenology and Hermeneutics: Experience, Meaning, and Interpretation

### Outcomes
- Read difficult philosophical texts with patience and conceptual precision.
- Analyze freedom, duty, history, self-consciousness, existence, experience, and interpretation.
- Develop an independent philosophical voice rather than merely summarize authorities.
- Use objections and replies to strengthen a sustained argument.

**Evidence of learning:** Close-reading notebook · philosophical analysis · oral defense · timed/untimed argument

**Alignment position:** Supports advanced 'doing philosophy' through concepts, argument, independent position-taking, and critical engagement.

## Philosophy 302 · Political Economy, Pragmatism & Liberation

**Level:** Advanced High School · Critical Thinking / College Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Marx, Materialism, Labor, Ideology, and Critical Theory; Pragmatism: Truth, Experience, Democracy, and Education; Decolonial, Black, Latin American, and Liberation Philosophies

### Outcomes
- Evaluate arguments about labor, ideology, democracy, experience, race, coloniality, and liberation.
- Distinguish evidence, assumptions, ideology critique, causal claims, and normative claims.
- Construct reasoned decisions from competing evidence and values.
- Write an argument that acknowledges material, historical, conceptual, and ethical dimensions.

**Evidence of learning:** Argument audit · ideology/source analysis · seminar · comparative position paper

**Alignment position:** Supports advanced critical-thinking, reasoning, evidence evaluation, problem analysis, and philosophical argument.

## Philosophy 303 · Mind, Language, Science, Technology & AI

**Level:** Advanced High School · College Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Analytic Philosophy: Language, Logic, Mind, and Ordinary Speech; Philosophy of Science, Technology, and AI

### Outcomes
- Analyze language, consciousness, explanation, evidence, scientific reasoning, technology, and AI.
- Distinguish conceptual questions from empirical questions and mixed questions.
- Test arguments with counterexamples and thought experiments.
- Build a contemporary philosophy portfolio using reliable research.

**Evidence of learning:** Thought-experiment portfolio · AI/science ethics brief · seminar · PROSE-edited research essay

**Alignment position:** Advanced reasoning and contemporary-issues bridge; not a claim of AP/IB/Cambridge program credit.

## Philosophy 401 · Logic, Argument & Epistemology

**Level:** College · Undergraduate Foundation

**Assigned resource modules:** Tools of Reasoning: Logic, Dialogue, and Argument; Jain, Nyaya, and Indian Logic: Nonviolence and Many-Sided Truth; Early Modern Rationalism: Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz; Early Modern Empiricism: Locke, Berkeley, Hume; Kant: Critique, Duty, Freedom, and the Conditions of Experience; Pragmatism: Truth, Experience, Democracy, and Education; Analytic Philosophy: Language, Logic, Mind, and Ordinary Speech

### Outcomes
- Reconstruct deductive and inductive arguments charitably.
- Evaluate validity, soundness, evidence, inference, justification, and counterexample.
- Compare epistemological methods across traditions.
- Write clear undergraduate philosophical prose with explicit argument structure.

**Evidence of learning:** Weekly argument maps · logic exercises · close reading · two papers · oral defense

**Alignment position:** Undergraduate foundation emphasizing critical reading, writing, speaking, argument construction/appraisal, and philosophical problems.

**Alignment position:** Undergraduate metaphysics/mind/religion pathway; institutions set their own credit and major requirements.

## Philosophy 601 · Advanced Seminar in Philosophical Historiography & Method

**Level:** Graduate-Style · Independent Scholar

**Assigned resource modules:** Tools of Reasoning: Logic, Dialogue, and Argument; Medieval Crossroads: Translation, Commentary, and Global Transmission; Renaissance Humanism, Skepticism, and New Worlds; Kant: Critique, Duty, Freedom, and the Conditions of Experience; German Idealism and Romanticism: Spirit, History, Freedom, and Art; Phenomenology and Hermeneutics: Experience, Meaning, and Interpretation; Analytic Philosophy: Language, Logic, Mind, and Ordinary Speech; Decolonial, Black, Latin American, and Liberation Philosophies

### Outcomes
- Frame a philosophical problem through competing methods and histories of interpretation.
- Analyze canon formation, translation, commentary, conceptual genealogy, and disciplinary boundaries.
- Synthesize scholarship rather than produce a sequence of summaries.
- Lead a graduate-style seminar and defend a methodological position.

**Evidence of learning:** Historiographic bibliography · method précis · seminar leadership · 15–20 page seminar paper

**Alignment position:** Graduate-style independent study; Khaemenes 600-series numbering is not institutional graduate credit.

## Philosophy 602 · Advanced Global Ethics, Knowledge & Political Thought

**Level:** Graduate-Style · Research Seminar

**Assigned resource modules:** Buddhist Philosophy: Suffering, Impermanence, No-Self, Compassion; Jain, Nyaya, and Indian Logic: Nonviolence and Many-Sided Truth; Confucius, Mencius, and Xunzi: Humaneness and Human Nature; African Philosophies: Ma'at, Ubuntu, Akan, Yoruba, Sage Wisdom; Indigenous Philosophies of the Americas: Land, Relation, Memory, and Responsibility; Enlightenment and Political Philosophy: Rights, Contract, Revolution, Critique; Utilitarianism, Liberalism, and Reform; Marx, Materialism, Labor, Ideology, and Critical Theory; Feminist Philosophy, Gender, Care, and Embodied Knowledge; Environmental and Animal Philosophy: Earth, Kinship, and Moral Community; Decolonial, Black, Latin American, and Liberation Philosophies

- Develop a bounded research problem across ethics, epistemology, politics, liberation, or ecological responsibility.

## Philosophy 603 · Philosophy Thesis · Comparative Wisdom & Public Practice

**Level:** Graduate-Style · Thesis / Capstone

**Assigned resource modules:** Capstone: Comparative Wisdom, Personal Canon, and Public Practice

### Outcomes
- Define an original, manageable philosophical problem.
- Build a primary and secondary reading list spanning the traditions actually relevant to the question.
- Develop multiple drafts through objections, replies, source review, and conceptual clarification.
- Defend the final project orally and connect philosophical inquiry to responsible public practice.

**Evidence of learning:** Proposal · annotated bibliography · staged drafts · thesis/project · oral defense · PROSE final edit

**Alignment position:** Highest Khaemenes Philosophy Hall pathway; graduate-style independent work, not an accredited thesis.
