# Curriculum Framework

## 33-Module World Classics & Humanities Spine

### 01 · Origins of Epic & Human Limits

**Primary works:** The Epic of Gilgamesh

**Focus:** Mortality, kingship, friendship, civilization, textual survival.

### 02 · War, Honor & Lament

**Primary works:** Iliad

**Focus:** Heroic code, rage, grief, warfare, divine/human causation, epic form.

### 03 · Homecoming, Identity & Storytelling

**Primary works:** Odyssey

**Focus:** Hospitality, identity, memory, narration, testing, return.

### 04 · Dharma, Kinship & Sacred Epic

**Primary works:** Mahabharata; Bhagavad Gita

**Focus:** Duty, moral conflict, family, war, sacred philosophy, embedded discourse.

### 05 · Exile, Devotion & Cultural Retelling

**Primary works:** Ramayana

**Focus:** Exile, kingship, devotion, gender, loyalty, adaptation across cultures.

### 06 · Persian Kingship & Cultural Memory

**Primary works:** Shahnameh

**Focus:** Heroism, justice, legitimacy, national memory, epic history.

### 07 · Maya Creation & Indigenous Cosmology

**Primary works:** Popol Vuh

**Focus:** Creation, ancestry, sacred narrative, colonization-era transmission, translation ethics.

### 08 · African Oral Epic & Performance

**Primary works:** Sundiata Epic; The Mwindo Epic

**Focus:** Oral history, performance, leadership, community, transcription, living tradition.

### 09 · Sacred Kingship & Ethiopian Memory

**Primary works:** Kebra Nagast

**Focus:** Lineage, covenant, nation, manuscript tradition, sacred history.

### 10 · Heroic Traditions Across Eurasia

**Primary works:** Beowulf; The Song of Roland; Nibelungenlied; The Book of Dede Korkut; Kalevala

**Focus:** Heroism, kinship, loyalty, frontier, oral tradition, national canon formation.

### 11 · War, Impermanence & Japanese Epic

**Primary works:** The Tale of the Heike

**Focus:** Buddhist impermanence, clan conflict, performance, grief, political collapse.

### 12 · Empire, Founding & Classical Inheritance

**Primary works:** Aeneid

**Focus:** Exile, duty, empire, founding myth, reception of Homer.

### 13 · Confucian Ethics & Education

**Primary works:** The Analects

**Focus:** Learning, virtue, ritual, governance, aphoristic form, commentary tradition.

### 14 · Daoism, Paradox & Perspective

**Primary works:** Tao Te Ching; Zhuangzi

**Focus:** Way, non-forcing, transformation, perspective, paradox, philosophical style.

### 15 · Stoic Self-Government

**Primary works:** Meditations; Enchiridion

**Focus:** Discipline, mortality, control, ethics, private writing, philosophical practice.

### 16 · Justice, Education & the Political Imagination

**Primary works:** Republic

**Focus:** Justice, education, truth, political order, dialogue, allegory.

### 17 · South Asian Philosophy & Liberation

**Primary works:** Upanishads; Dhammapada

**Focus:** Self, mind, suffering, knowledge, liberation, ethical discipline.

### 18 · Strategy, Power & Political Realism

**Primary works:** The Art of War; The Prince

**Focus:** Power, conflict, strategy, leadership, prudence, rhetoric, historical use.

### 19 · Confession, Memory & the Self

**Primary works:** Confessions

**Focus:** Autobiography, memory, conversion, desire, time, interiority.

### 20 · Greek Tragedy & Civic Conflict

**Primary works:** Oedipus Rex; Antigone; The Bacchae

**Focus:** Fate, law, kinship, knowledge, ritual, civic order, tragic form.

### 21 · Drama Beyond Europe

**Primary works:** The Recognition of Śakuntalā; The Peony Pavilion

**Focus:** Love, memory, performance, aesthetics, court culture, translation.

### 22 · Shakespearean Tragedy

**Primary works:** Hamlet; King Lear

**Focus:** Character, uncertainty, language, power, family, madness, performance history.

### 23 · Modern Drama & Social Order

**Primary works:** Faust; A Doll's House; The Cherry Orchard

**Focus:** Modernity, selfhood, gender, class, change, realism, dramatic structure.

### 24 · Medieval & Early Modern European Poetic Worlds

**Primary works:** Divine Comedy; Canterbury Tales; Paradise Lost

**Focus:** Pilgrimage, theology, satire, epic inheritance, voice, cosmology.

### 25 · Persian Mystical & Philosophical Poetry

**Primary works:** Masnavi; Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam; The Conference of the Birds

**Focus:** Mysticism, doubt, allegory, lyric form, translation and reception.

### 26 · Chinese Classical Poetry & the Lyric Tradition

**Primary works:** The Book of Songs; Li Bai Selected Poems; Du Fu Selected Poems

**Focus:** Image, voice, history, nature, social witness, translation.

### 27 · Poetry, Nation & Modern Voice

**Primary works:** Leaves of Grass; Gitanjali; Martín Fierro

**Focus:** Nation, self, spirituality, frontier, voice, modern poetic identity.

### 28 · Metamorphosis, Myth & Literary Afterlife

**Primary works:** Metamorphoses

**Focus:** Transformation, mythic structure, intertextuality, adaptation, classical reception.

### 29 · Global Story Worlds Before the Modern Novel

**Primary works:** The Tale of Genji; Journey to the West; One Thousand and One Nights; The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter; The Pillow Book

**Focus:** Frame narrative, court writing, travel, fantasy, episodic form, prose traditions.

### 30 · The Novel, Satire & Social Worlds

**Primary works:** Don Quixote; Pride and Prejudice

**Focus:** Narrative self-awareness, satire, class, reading, marriage, social judgment.

### 31 · Gothic, Romantic & Interior Worlds

**Primary works:** Frankenstein; Jane Eyre; Wuthering Heights

**Focus:** Creation, desire, gender, isolation, landscape, narration, Gothic form.

### 32 · Realism, Society & Moral Crisis

**Primary works:** The Count of Monte Cristo; Les Misérables; Madame Bovary; Anna Karenina; War and Peace; Crime and Punishment; The Brothers Karamazov

**Focus:** Justice, class, revolution, desire, family, psychology, morality, realism.

### 33 · Modernity, Testimony & Global Revisions

**Primary works:** Moby-Dick; The Scarlet Letter; Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass; Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl; Dream of the Red Chamber; Things Fall Apart; The Trial; Pedro Páramo; The Tale of Kieu; Ifá Divination Verses; The Forest of a Thousand Daemons

**Focus:** Modernity, colonialism, testimony, race, law, memory, oral/written tradition, form, decolonization, world-literature canon.

## Leveling the Same Module

### Grades 9–10
Use close reading, accurate summary, strong textual evidence, theme, characterization, structure, context, comparison, and organized analytical writing.

### Grades 11–12
Add ambiguity, multiple themes, intertextuality, rhetorical analysis, independent complex reading, sustained argument, research, source synthesis, and oral presentation.

### Advanced High School
Add timed/unseen analysis where appropriate, genre-specific literary analysis, comparative essays, rhetoric, oral commentary/presentation, and sustained independent study.

### University 100–200
Add scholarly-source discovery, source evaluation, annotated bibliography, MLA-style documentation, secondary criticism, comparative research, and seminar participation.

### University 300–400
Add theory/method, translation/reception, historiography of criticism, advanced bibliography, seminar leadership, and a substantial independent capstone.
