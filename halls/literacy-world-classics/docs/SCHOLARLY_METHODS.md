# Scholarly Methods

## 1. ◉ Close Reading

Analyze diction, syntax, imagery, figurative language, detail, pattern, ambiguity, and textual evidence.

## 2. ⌛ Historical & Cultural Context

Situate texts in periods, traditions, institutions, conflicts, beliefs, and material conditions without reducing literature to background.

## 3. ◇ Genre, Form & Structure

Study epic, lyric, drama, novel, philosophy, oral tradition, sacred writing, frame tale, narrative structure, and formal convention.

## 4. ¶ Rhetoric, Voice & Style

Analyze speaker/narrator, audience, purpose, claims, appeals, tone, style, irony, and rhetorical choices.

## 5. ⇄ Comparative Literature

Compare works across cultures, periods, genres, languages, adaptations, themes, and intellectual traditions.

## 6. 文 Language & Translation

Consider translation choices, untranslatable concepts, editions, textual transmission, multilingual context, and reception.

## 7. ? Research Questions

Develop focused, contestable, researchable literary questions and refine them as evidence changes the inquiry.

## 8. ▦ Source Evaluation

Distinguish primary, secondary, reference, popular, and scholarly sources; evaluate authority, relevance, method, evidence, and limitation.

## 9. § Citation & Scholarly Conversation

Quote, paraphrase, cite, synthesize, build annotated bibliographies, and position an argument in relation to criticism.

## 10. ◎ Seminar & Oral Argument

Prepare, discuss, question, listen, present, respond to counterarguments, and lead evidence-based conversation.

## 11. ✦ Synthesis & Capstone

Integrate primary texts, scholarship, context, method, and independent argument into a substantial final project.

## Principle

The Hall tracks skills separately.

A learner can demonstrate advanced close reading while still developing research-source evaluation, or strong seminar discussion while still developing documentation and scholarly citation.

A single composite “humanities score” is therefore intentionally avoided.
