# University Alignment

There is no single national university literature curriculum.

For that reason, the Hall does **not** claim universal college-credit equivalence.

Instead, the university pathways are organized around scholarly practices common to undergraduate literary and humanities study.

## University 100–200 Level

Expected practices:

- accurate close reading;
- historical and cultural context;
- genre/form analysis;
- comparative reading;
- research questions;
- primary vs. secondary source distinctions;
- scholarly-source discovery;
- source evaluation;
- annotated bibliography;
- quotation and paraphrase;
- MLA-style documentation;
- literary criticism;
- seminar discussion;
- analytical and comparative research writing.

## University 300–400 Level

Expected practices:

- independent primary-text selection;
- advanced close reading;
- history of scholarship;
- theory and method;
- translation studies;
- reception history;
- textual/material history;
- advanced bibliographic research;
- scholarly conversation;
- seminar leadership;
- conference-style oral presentation;
- substantial independent research / capstone.

## MLA Research Alignment

The Hall’s university pathway is informed by MLA resources on undergraduate literature research, including:

- distinguishing general information from scholarship;
- evaluating sources;
- using literary criticism;
- understanding research as a scholarly conversation;
- advanced database searching;
- using textual evidence to support original analysis;
- interdisciplinary world-literature research.
