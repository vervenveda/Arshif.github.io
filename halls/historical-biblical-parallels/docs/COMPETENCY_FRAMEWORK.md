# Competency Framework

## 1. § Close Reading & Genre

Identify what a text says, how it says it, its literary form, rhetoric, audience, internal claims and interpretive limits.

## 2. ⌂ Historical Method & Source Classification

Distinguish primary/secondary/tertiary sources, chronology, date, authorship, purpose, audience and evidentiary weight.

## 3. ◫ Archaeology & Material Culture

Use context, stratigraphy, assemblage, architecture, objects and excavation/survey evidence without overclaiming.

## 4. 𐤀 Epigraphy, Papyri, Coins & Administrative Texts

Work with inscriptions, papyri, seals, coins and archives through provenance, transcription/translation and genre.

## 5. ⌖ Chronology, Geography & Political Context

Use dates, synchronisms, routes, landscape, cities, empires, provinces and institutions accurately.

## 6. ≋ Manuscripts, Textual Criticism & Translation

Compare witnesses, variants and translation traditions while making editorial uncertainty visible.

## 7. ◇ Second Temple & Greco-Roman Context

Understand Jewish diversity, Hellenistic/Roman institutions, ancient writers and New Testament historical settings.

## 8. ⚖ Historiography, Debate & Evidence Limits

Map scholarly agreement/disagreement and distinguish direct evidence, inference, disputed inference and speculation.

## 9. ☼ Religious Literacy & Tradition Labeling

Describe Jewish and Christian canons, practices and interpretations accurately without treating one tradition as universal.

## 10. ▦ Research, Citation & Digital Source Trails

Build questions, bibliographies, research logs, metadata, museum/archive trails and reproducible digital evidence systems.

## 11. ✦ Synthesis, Faith Reflection & Capstone

Integrate text, history and evidence while clearly distinguishing historical conclusions from devotional/theological reflection.

The Hall intentionally does not calculate a single “Bible proof score” or “faith score.”
