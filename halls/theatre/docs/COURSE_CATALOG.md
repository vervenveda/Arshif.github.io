# Theatre 101–603 Course Catalog

## Theatre 101 · Story, Stage & Ensemble Foundations

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 6

**Assigned modules:** Origins of Theatre; Monologue Technique; Scene Study; Acting Foundations · Given Circumstances, Objective, Tactics & Listening; Ensemble, Devising & Collaborative Creation; Rehearsal Safety, Consent, Intimacy & Stage-Combat Boundaries

### Outcomes
- Explain how story, performer, audience, space and community create theatre.
- Use objective, obstacle, action, beat and listening in age-appropriate scene work.
- Create and revise original ensemble material rather than copying protected scripts.
- Use consent, rehearsal boundaries and safe collaborative habits.

**Evidence:** Theatre journal · ensemble exercise · scene map · short performance/reflection

**Alignment / boundary:** National Core Arts Standards middle-school processes: Creating, Performing, Responding and Connecting.

## Theatre 102 · World Theatre Traditions & Performance Forms

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 7

**Assigned modules:** Greek Tragedy; Greek Comedy & Satire; Roman Comedy; Sanskrit Drama; Chinese Theatre Traditions; Japanese Noh & Kabuki; Medieval Mystery & Morality Plays; Commedia dell’arte

### Outcomes
- Compare Greek, Roman, Sanskrit, Chinese, Japanese, medieval European and commedia traditions.
- Use specific historical/cultural context rather than treating world theatre as one style.
- Identify chorus, mask, gesture, stock character, music, ritual, satire and formal convention.
- Respond to performance choices using evidence and theatre vocabulary.

**Evidence:** Tradition cards · staging comparison · design response · seminar

**Alignment / boundary:** Middle-school theatre history, cultural context, creation, performance and response.

## Theatre 103 · Script to Stage · Character, Design & Playmaking

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 8

**Assigned modules:** Elizabethan & Jacobean Stage; Spanish Golden Age; French Neoclassicism & Comedy; Monologue Technique; Scene Study; Playwriting Structure; Design: Set, Costume, Light, Sound; Rights, Royalties & Ethics; Capstone: Build a Theatre Night

### Outcomes
- Analyze character, conflict, structure, language and stage directions.
- Connect design choices to theme and practical production needs.
- Draft an original short script and revise from feedback.
- Distinguish script-reading access from performance/adaptation rights.

**Evidence:** Script analysis · design concept · original short scene · rights checklist

**Alignment / boundary:** Grade 8 / high-school-readiness creating, performing, responding and connecting.

## Theatre 201 · Global Theatre History & Dramatic Literature

**Level:** High School · Proficient

**Assigned modules:** Origins of Theatre; Greek Tragedy; Greek Comedy & Satire; Roman Comedy; Sanskrit Drama; Chinese Theatre Traditions; Japanese Noh & Kabuki; Medieval Mystery & Morality Plays; African & Diaspora Theatre; Indigenous & Living Performance Traditions

### Outcomes
- Analyze theatre works within historical, social, religious, political and performance contexts.
- Compare dramatic forms without flattening distinct traditions.
- Use primary/public-domain texts, licensed scripts and reliable theatre scholarship appropriately.
- Connect textual evidence to staging and audience experience.

**Evidence:** Reading notebook · context dossier · scene presentation · analytical essay

**Alignment / boundary:** National Core Arts Standards HS Proficient; supports Creating, Performing, Responding and Connecting.

## Theatre 202 · Early Modern Stage, Style & Performance

**Level:** High School · Proficient / Accomplished

**Assigned modules:** Commedia dell’arte; Elizabethan & Jacobean Stage; Spanish Golden Age; French Neoclassicism & Comedy; Restoration & Comedy of Manners; Monologue Technique; Scene Study; Design: Set, Costume, Light, Sound

### Outcomes
- Study Elizabethan/Jacobean, Spanish Golden Age, French neoclassical, Restoration and related performance contexts.
- Analyze verse/prose, status, rhetoric, comedy, tragedy and theatrical convention.

- Revise performance after peer/teacher feedback.

**Evidence:** Text marking · scene study · design response · reflection

**Alignment / boundary:** HS Proficient→Accomplished theatre performance and interpretation.

## Theatre 203 · Modern, Social & Contemporary Theatre

**Level:** High School · Accomplished / Advanced

**Assigned modules:** Melodrama, Romanticism & 19th Century Forms; Realism & Naturalism; Symbolism & Expressionism; Irish, American & Early Modern Social Drama; African & Diaspora Theatre; Indigenous & Living Performance Traditions; Absurdism & Postwar Theatre; Contemporary New Work; Rights, Royalties & Ethics; Rehearsal Safety, Consent, Intimacy & Stage-Combat Boundaries

### Outcomes
- Compare realism, naturalism, symbolism, expressionism, social drama, African/diaspora theatre, Indigenous/living traditions, absurdism and contemporary new work.
- Analyze power, representation, authorship, cultural protocol and production context.
- Create or perform substantial work using research and reflective evaluation.
- Use rights and consent practices appropriate to living playwrights and communities.

**Evidence:** Comparative dossier · performance project · context-first discussion guide · 6–8 page paper

**Alignment / boundary:** HS Accomplished/Advanced; college-preparatory theatre inquiry.
