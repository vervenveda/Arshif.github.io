# Reading Framework

# Khaemenes Reading & Literacy Hall · Preschool–Grade 8

The Hall is organized around a developmental progression rather than a single reading score.

## 1. Preschool · Oral Language & Emergent Literacy

Primary goals:

- conversation and listening;
- expressive and receptive vocabulary;
- narrative language;
- rhyme and syllable awareness;
- beginning phoneme awareness;
- alphabet knowledge;
- print concepts;
- rich read-aloud comprehension;
- storytelling;
- drawing, dictation, and early writing.

### Instructional principle

Preschool should build strong language and literacy foundations without reducing early childhood to worksheet-driven decoding.

---

## 2. Kindergarten–Grade 2 · Learn to Read

Primary goals:

- phonemic awareness;
- systematic phonics;
- decoding;
- encoding and spelling;
- common irregular words;
- increasingly complex word patterns;
- fluency;
- vocabulary;
- literature and informational comprehension;
- discussion;
- narrative, informational, and opinion writing.

### Instructional principle

Beginning readers need rich language and knowledge **and** explicit instruction in the written code.

Learner-readable practice should align to sound-spelling patterns that have already been taught.

---

## 3. Grades 3–5 · Read to Learn

Primary goals:

- multisyllabic decoding;
- syllable patterns;
- morphology;
- prefixes, suffixes, and roots;
- fluent independent reading;
- vocabulary;
- theme and summary;
- point of view;
- informational text structures;
- text features;
- evidence;
- writing from sources;
- short research.

### Instructional principle

Difficulty with decoding or spelling should not automatically be treated as a problem that the learner will simply outgrow.

Older elementary learners may still need explicit word-reading support.

---

## 4. Grades 6–8 · Analyze & Synthesize

Primary goals:

- complex literature;
- sustained nonfiction;
- disciplinary reading;
- academic vocabulary;
- morphology and etymology;
- textual evidence;
- inference;
- argument;
- source evaluation;
- corroboration;
- synthesis across sources and media;
- research;
- citation;
- evidence-based writing;
- seminars and presentation.

### Instructional principle

Middle-school literacy is broader than “reading novels.”

Learners should increasingly read the kinds of texts used in:

- English/literature;
- history/social studies;
- science;
- technical subjects;
- civic life;
- digital/media environments.

---

# Eleven Literacy Strands

1. Oral Language & Listening
2. Phonological / Phonemic Awareness
3. Phonics & Decoding
4. Encoding, Spelling & Word Study
5. Fluency & Reading Stamina
6. Vocabulary & Morphology
7. Literary Comprehension
8. Informational / Disciplinary Reading
9. Evidence, Analysis & Research
10. Writing From Reading
11. Speaking, Discussion & Presentation

The Hall intentionally keeps these separate because learner development is rarely uniform across every strand.

---

# Literature and Foundational Reading

The Hall includes a large literature collection.

That literature is used to develop:

- oral language;
- knowledge;
- vocabulary;
- comprehension;
- literary understanding;
- cultural knowledge;
- discussion;
- writing;
- imagination;
- reader identity.

For beginning readers, however, rich literature alone is not a decoding curriculum.

The Hall therefore distinguishes:

- **texts learners hear or study for knowledge and literature**, and
- **texts learners read independently to practise taught decoding patterns**.

---

# Progress Use

Skill observations are intended to answer:

- What appears secure?
- What is still developing?
- What has not been observed?
- What should be taught or reviewed next?
- What evidence should NAIB, a mentor, teacher, or family review?

They should not answer:

- How intelligent is this learner?
- Is this learner “good” or “bad” at reading?
- What diagnosis does this learner have?

Those questions require different evidence and, where appropriate, qualified professional assessment.
