# Research Ethics & Indigenous Data Sovereignty

This document summarizes the framework architecture used by the Hall. It is educational guidance, not legal advice or community authorization.

## CARE Principles
Global Indigenous Data Alliance (GIDA) frames CARE as:
- Collective Benefit
- Authority to Control
- Responsibility
- Ethics

CARE is people- and purpose-oriented and is designed to address rights and power questions not solved by technical openness alone.

Official source:
https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples

## OCAP®
The First Nations Information Governance Centre describes OCAP® as:
- Ownership
- Control
- Access
- Possession

OCAP® concerns First Nations data governance and should not be relabeled as a universal framework for every Indigenous people.

Official source:
https://fnigc.ca/ocap-training/

## Local Contexts
Traditional Knowledge (TK) and Biocultural (BC) Labels are community tools for expressing community-specific responsibilities and conditions around access, sharing and reuse.

The Hall may teach how to recognize/use the framework; it does not authorize an outside learner to assign labels on behalf of a community.

Official source:
https://localcontexts.org/labels/about-the-labels/

## AIATSIS
For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander research, the AIATSIS ethics framework emphasizes:
1. Indigenous self-determination
2. Indigenous leadership
3. Impact and value
4. Sustainability and accountability

It also addresses engagement, informed consent, Indigenous knowledge/data, benefit, risk, land/waters, governance and reporting.

Official source:
https://aiatsis.gov.au/research/ethical-research

## UNDRIP
The United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples recognizes rights connected to:
- cultural heritage;
- traditional knowledge;
- cultural expressions;
- languages;
- oral traditions;
- spiritual/religious traditions;
- Indigenous education and cultural institutions.

Official source:
https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/ga_61-295/ga_61-295.html

## Core Design Rule
Do not convert “open access” into a claim that Indigenous knowledge is free for unrestricted reuse.
