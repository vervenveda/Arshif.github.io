# Indigenous Oral Traditions & Storywork 101–603 Course Catalog

## IOT 101 · Respectful Listening & Oral Tradition Foundations

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 6

**Assigned resource modules:** What Is an Oral Tradition?; Protocols Before Content; Language as Worldview; Visual Memory Systems

### Outcomes
- Define oral tradition, oral history, storywork, protocol, and living tradition.
- Distinguish public learning from restricted or community-governed knowledge.
- Identify people, place, language, speaker/source, and version before interpretation.
- Use listening, memory, image, and language as evidence without treating oral knowledge as inferior to print.

**Evidence of learning:** Listening journal · source/context card · protocol reflection · visual-memory analysis

**Alignment / boundary:** Grade 6 ELA/history literacy: evidence, central ideas, source distinction, speaking/listening, and accurate context.

## IOT 102 · Story, History, Performance & Place

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 7

**Assigned resource modules:** Oral Tradition as Historical Evidence; Performance as Archive; Turtle Island / North America Survey; Mesoamerica and Central America; Arctic Oral Cartography

### Outcomes
- Explain how oral tradition can carry historical knowledge.
- Compare spoken, visual, performed, and written records.
- Analyze how place and community shape meaning.
- Avoid pan-Indigenous generalization by naming specific peoples and sources.

**Evidence of learning:** Evidence comparison · place/context map · performance analysis · short inquiry response

**Alignment / boundary:** Grade 7 source analysis, point of view, comparison of media/forms, research, and evidence-based discussion.

## IOT 103 · Knowledge Systems, Ecology & Ethical Comparison

**Level:** Middle School · Grade 8

**Assigned resource modules:** Story as Law and Governance; Traditional Ecological Knowledge and Story; Water, Rivers, and Sea Memory; Fire, Land Care, and Seasonal Knowledge; Sky Knowledge and Timekeeping; Foodways and Plant Memory

### Outcomes
- Analyze relationships among story, law, ecology, seasonality, foodways, land, and water.
- Compare traditions without claiming equivalence or flattening difference.
- Use strongest relevant public evidence for interpretation.
- Explain why community authority can limit what a classroom should reproduce.

**Evidence of learning:** Comparative inquiry · ecology/knowledge chart · evidence-based presentation · protocol audit

**Alignment / boundary:** Grade 8 source evaluation, strongest evidence, multiple-source comparison, research, and responsible presentation.

## IOT 201 · Oral History, Source Criticism & Colonial Records

**Level:** High School · Grades 9–10

**Assigned resource modules:** What Is an Oral Tradition?; Protocols Before Content; Source Criticism and Colonial Records; Oral Tradition as Historical Evidence; Language as Worldview; Visual Memory Systems; Performance as Archive

### Outcomes
- Evaluate date, origin, mediation, translation, and power in primary/secondary sources.
- Compare colonial transcriptions with community-centered or living sources when available.
- Separate what a source actually supports from assumption or inherited textbook framing.
- Write evidence-based historical/literary analysis.

**Evidence of learning:** Source-criticism dossier · paired-source analysis · seminar · analytical essay

**Alignment / boundary:** Grades 9–10 history/social-studies literacy: source origin, evidence, point of view, comparison, reasoning, and independent comprehension.

## IOT 202 · World Indigenous Traditions · Regional Study

**Level:** High School · Grades 9–10

**Assigned resource modules:** Turtle Island / North America Survey; Mesoamerica and Central America; South America and Amazonia; Arctic Oral Cartography; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Knowledge; Pacific Genealogies and Chants; African Oral Intellectual Traditions; Ainu, Adivasi, Siberian, and Asian Indigenous Traditions

### Outcomes
- Study regions through specific communities rather than generic continental labels.
- Compare language, genre, land relationship, oral transmission, and public-source practices.
- Recognize continuity, change, colonial disruption, revitalization, and contemporary Indigenous life.
- Build a documented comparative study without appropriating restricted material.

**Evidence of learning:** Regional dossier set · comparative presentation · map/timeline · PROSE-edited research response

**Alignment / boundary:** Grades 9–10 world-history/ELA inquiry, comparison of sources, explanatory writing, discussion, and research.

## IOT 203 · Law, Kinship, Land & Indigenous Knowledge Systems

**Level:** High School · Grades 11–12

**Assigned resource modules:** Story as Law and Governance; Genealogy, Ancestors, and Kinship; Traditional Ecological Knowledge and Story; Water, Rivers, and Sea Memory; Fire, Land Care, and Seasonal Knowledge; Sky Knowledge and Timekeeping; Foodways and Plant Memory; Diaspora, Exile, and Survival

### Outcomes

## IOT 301 · Decolonizing the Archive & Digital Ethics

**Level:** Advanced High School · College Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Source Criticism and Colonial Records; Visual Memory Systems; Performance as Archive; Digital Archives Without Extraction; Protocols Before Content; Community Partnership and Reciprocity

### Outcomes
- Analyze how archives, catalogues, collectors, translations, and metadata can reproduce colonial power.
- Design access levels, permission notes, provenance, correction, and takedown pathways.
- Compare openness with cultural governance and community authority.
- Apply ethical digital-humanities reasoning to a small public-source archive.

**Evidence of learning:** Archive audit · metadata schema · access policy · digital-ethics presentation

**Alignment / boundary:** Advanced research/information-literacy bridge with Indigenous data-governance safeguards.

## IOT 302 · Community Partnership, Reciprocity & Public Humanities

**Level:** Advanced High School · College Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Protocols Before Content; Teaching Indigenous Oral Traditions Responsibly; Community Partnership and Reciprocity; Creative Retelling Ethics; Reflection: Listening as Responsibility

- Distinguish consultation, collaboration, co-design, permission, and community governance.
- Plan credit, benefit, compensation, review, correction, and reciprocity.
- Design public-facing learning that avoids ceremony imitation and pan-Indigenous aesthetics.
- Reflect on researcher/teacher position, limits, and responsibility.

**Evidence of learning:** Partnership plan · ethical lesson · public-humanities proposal · reflexive statement

**Alignment / boundary:** Advanced inquiry and public-humanities preparation; community authority overrides classroom convenience.

## IOT 303 · Oral Tradition Research Capstone

**Level:** Advanced High School · College Bridge

**Assigned resource modules:** Capstone: Oral Tradition Atlas; Capstone: Responsible Lesson Plan; Capstone: Ethical Mini Archive; Reflection: Listening as Responsibility; Digital Archives Without Extraction

### Outcomes

- Document sources, permissions, access status, limitations, and community-specific protocols.

**Alignment / boundary:** College-bridge capstone; not an external credential or permission to conduct human-subject/community research.

**Assigned resource modules:** Capstone: Oral Tradition Atlas; Capstone: Responsible Lesson Plan; Capstone: Ethical Mini Archive; Reflection: Listening as Responsibility; Community Partnership and Reciprocity; Digital Archives Without Extraction
