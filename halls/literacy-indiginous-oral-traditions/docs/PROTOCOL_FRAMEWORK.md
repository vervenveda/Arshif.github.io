# Protocol Framework

## Core Rule
Community-specific authority comes before a generic classroom rule.

## Eleven Safety Rails

1. Permission before preservation
2. Public is not free-for-all
3. Specificity is respect
4. Do not imitate ceremony
5. Credit living voices
6. Leave space for silence
7. Reciprocity matters
8. Archive with governance
9. Frameworks have scope
10. Research is not just a school assignment
11. AI cannot grant cultural permission

## Public Learning vs. Research

A learner may analyze public, community-approved sources without automatically conducting human-subject research.

A project moves into a different ethical category when it involves:
- interviewing people;
- recording stories;
- collecting nonpublic community knowledge;
- creating datasets about Indigenous people;
- publishing community-governed information;
- storing restricted or culturally sensitive material;
- making reuse decisions on behalf of a community.

At that point, institutional and community-specific approval processes may apply.

## Silence as a Valid Result
A high-quality project may conclude:
- this source is insufficient;
- this material should not be reproduced;
- permission is unclear;
- community review is needed;
- a question should remain unanswered publicly.

Those are academically meaningful outcomes.
