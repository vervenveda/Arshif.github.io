# Assessment Framework

The Hall avoids one composite “Indigenous knowledge score.”

## Eleven Competencies
- Protocol, Authority & Consent
- People, Place, Language & Specificity
- Listening, Memory & Oral Evidence
- Language, Translation & Transcription
- Source Criticism & Historiography
- Land, Ecology, Law & Kinship
- Performance, Visual & Material Memory
- Comparative Method Without Flattening
- Reciprocity, Partnership & Reflexivity
- Data Sovereignty, Archives & Cultural Rights
- Teaching, Public Humanities & Capstone

Each competency may be:
- Not observed
- Developing
- Secure

## Evidence
Appropriate evidence may include:
- listening/source journals;
- source criticism;
- public-source comparison;
- research questions;
- metadata/access audits;
- ethical lesson plans;
- annotated bibliographies;
- seminar discussion;
- policy/governance memos;
- public-humanities plans;
- reflective positionality statements;
- capstones using authorized/public materials.

## Important Boundary
A “secure” competency does not mean the learner has cultural authority to teach, perform, archive, translate, or disclose community knowledge.
